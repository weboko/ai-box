Self-host Firecracker on Hetzner, or rent E2B / Modal?

A sandbox decision for a 2-person dev-tools startup. Costs, cold starts and the labor nobody prices in.

About this report

Reading the margin

The short answer

Rent: E2B first. Self-hosting only wins on hardware; once your own time is priced in, the break-even moves from ~500 to ~13,000–32,000 sandbox-hours a month.

Two Hetzner AX42 boxes cost about as much as ~490 sandbox-hours a month on E2B Pro. But Firecracker itself is the easy part: networking, snapshots, scheduling and an in-VM agent are 12–18 engineer-months for a production platform, and ops labor dominates below a few thousand sandbox-hours a month. For a two-person team that is the whole company for half a year.

Start on E2B (Firecracker isolation, pause/resume) or Modal Starter (cheapest below ~2,070 h/month); keep an exit path; re-check at ~$5k/month of sandbox spend.

  2. Labor flips it. Adding 20 ops-hours/month pushes break-even to ~13,000 h/month (~18 sandboxes running 24/7); adding a 4-engineer-month build pushes it to ~32,000 h/month.
  3. Hetzner got ~2× more expensive for new orders on 15 June 2026. AX42-1 is now €97.30/month + €49 setup; cloud CPX42 went €25.49 → €69.49. Blog break-evens using pre-June prices are stale.
  4. Hetzner Cloud VMs are not an option for Firecracker. Firecracker needs /dev/kvm, which requires nested virtualization on cloud VMs; plan on dedicated AX servers, where KVM works. Hetzner does not document nested virt on Cloud.
  5. Cold-start numbers disagree by ~5× (150 ms claimed vs 264–717 ms measured) because they measure different things; for agent UX the gap is small. See cold starts.
  6. Modal vs E2B is a fee-vs-rate trade. Modal costs $0.238/h for 2 vCPU / 4 GiB vs E2B's $0.166/h, but has no plan fee; E2B Hobby's 1-hour session and 20-sandbox caps push real use to Pro at $150/month. Crossover ≈ 2,070 h/month.

What is being compared

Three ways to give an agent a disposable Linux box: E2B (Firecracker microVMs), Modal (gVisor containers), or your own Firecracker fleet.

E2B runs each sandbox as a Firecracker microVM with its own kernel. Modal Sandboxes default to gVisor, a user-space kernel that intercepts syscalls; VMs are an alternative. Self-hosting means running Firecracker under its jailer on KVM-capable bare metal. On the self-host path, you own every box in the diagram below.

Isolation

Firecracker: hardware virtualization boundary, with the jailer as “a second line of defense”. gVisor: stronger than namespaces, lighter than a VM. Self-host gets E2B-grade isolation only if you keep the seccomp/jailer config tight: “a permanent line item”.

Limits

E2B Hobby: 1 h sessions, 20 concurrent. Pro: 24 h, 100 concurrent, $150/month. Enterprise: $3,000/month minimum, BYOC available. Modal: default sandbox lifetime 5 min, max 24 h. Starter: 100 containers; Team: 5,000 containers at $250/month.

The numbers

At 2,000 sandbox-hours a month, E2B Pro costs $481 and Modal $476. A self-hosted fleet costs $230 in hardware, or $4,897 once your time is counted.

The reference sandbox is 2 vCPU / 4 GiB. One costs $0.1656/h on E2B and $0.2380/h on Modal. Self-hosting is a fixed cost with ~$0 marginal until the box is full: an AX42 fits 14 sandboxes, RAM-bound, and you need at least two boxes. Firecracker's own overhead, under 5 MiB per VM, is negligible.

When does self-hosting pay off?

One camp says ~400 sandbox-hours a month. The other says labor dominates. Both are right about different cost lines.

The hardware view

Break-even at ~400 sandbox-hours/month (“roughly one default sandbox running half the day”) on a ~€64 AX52 vs E2B. A companion post puts it near two dozen runs a day, on an 8-vCPU/16-GB Hetzner cloud box at ~$19/month with ~$0 marginal cost.

The labor view

Ops labor is “the dominant line below a few thousand sandbox-hours a month”. Building a production platform is “comfortably 12–18 engineer-months”, from an author who sells a sandbox product.

Reconciliation

  1. Re-price the hardware. Side 1's prices predate Hetzner's 15 June 2026 repricing: the $19 cloud box (CPX42-class) is now €69.49, and Hetzner Cloud lacks documented KVM anyway. With 2 × AX42 at €97.30: (230.47 − 150) ÷ 0.1656 ≈ 486 h/month. The hardware conclusion survives the price hike.
  2. Price the labor. Side 1 prices it at zero. Add just 20 h/month at $100/h: (2,230.47 − 150) ÷ 0.1656 ≈ 12,560 h → with fleet sizing ≈ 13,260 h/month.
  3. Conclusion. For a 2-person team, labor is not a rounding error; it is the scarcest input. Self-hosting pays only above roughly 13k–32k sandbox-hours/month, i.e. an E2B bill of ~$2.3k–5.5k/month.

How fast is a cold start, really?

Claims say 125–150 ms; measurements say 264–717 ms. They time different things: budget ~0.3–0.7 s for E2B.

Firecracker boots “in <125 ms”; E2B is commonly quoted at ~150 ms. Benchmarks that time create → first command tell a different story.

Why they disagree

“<125 ms” is VMM-to-guest boot on AWS's tuned path; benchmarks time API call → network → scheduling → first exec, so they must be higher. The 2.7× gap between the two E2B measurements is unexplained (region, client location, method).

Conclusion. Budget ~0.3–0.7 s for E2B create-to-first-command. Self-host only beats it with a snapshot-restore warm pool (~28 ms restore), which is one of the 12–18 eng-months items. Cold start does not decide this question.

Recommendation

Rent now, behind a thin interface. Re-check when the bill says so.

  1. Now: E2B Pro, or Modal Starter while under ~2,000 h/month and if 24-hour sessions without pause are fine. Choose E2B if you need pause/resume or microVM isolation for untrusted user code; Modal if you also need GPUs.
  2. Keep the exit open. Wrap sandbox calls behind a thin interface (create / exec / files / pause / kill) so a switch costs days, not months.
  3. Re-evaluate when sandbox spend passes ~$5k/month for 3 consecutive months, you hit E2B Pro's 100-concurrent cap, or a customer requires data residency / BYOC (E2B Enterprise starts at $3,000/month).
  4. If you do self-host later: dedicated AX servers, not Hetzner Cloud; budget the 8 components, not just Firecracker.

Risks & unknowns

Prices and caps can move this decision before volume does.

  • Prices move fast: Hetzner raised twice in 2026; vendor rates may also change.
  • Concurrency limits, not price, may force the decision first (E2B Pro 100; Modal Starter 100).
  • Build-effort figures come from a vendor with a conflict of interest; no neutral estimate found.
  • Idle-heavy agents: billed hours depend on pausing aggressively; a sandbox that “computes 10 minutes of each hour but lives the full hour costs 6×”.
  • Hetzner Cloud nested virtualization status is undocumented.

Next steps

  1. Instrument real usage. Sandbox-hours/month and peak concurrency, for 2 weeks.
