Overview

TL;DR

Rent (E2B first). Self-hosting only wins on hardware; once your own time is priced in, the break-even moves from ~500 to ~13,000–32,000 sandbox-hours a month.

At 2,000 h/month the cheapest is Modal Starter: E2B Pro $481, Modal Starter $476, self-host all-in $4,897

The short version

Two Hetzner AX42 boxes cost about as much as ~490 sandbox-hours/month on E2B Pro. But Firecracker itself is the easy part: networking, snapshots, scheduling and an in-VM agent are 12–18 engineer-months for a production platform, and ops labor dominates below a few thousand sandbox-hours a month. For a two-person team that is the whole company for half a year.

Start on E2B (Firecracker isolation, pause/resume) or Modal Starter (cheapest below ~2,070 h/month); keep an exit path; re-check at ~$5k/month of sandbox spend.

Isolation

E2B: Firecracker microVM, own kernel. Modal: gVisor by default. Self-host: Firecracker under jailer on KVM.

Three ways to give an agent a disposable Linux box: E2B (Firecracker microVMs), Modal (gVisor containers), or your own Firecracker fleet.

  • Firecracker: hardware virtualization boundary, jailer as “a second line of defense”.
  • gVisor: stronger than namespaces, lighter than a VM. VMs are an alternative on Modal.
  • Self-host: E2B-grade isolation only if you keep seccomp/jailer config tight: “a permanent line item”.

Claims

Firecracker boots “in <125ms”; E2B is commonly quoted at ~150 ms.

Measured (create → first command)

E2B median 264 ms, p95 603 ms (Lizard); 717 ms (LogRocket).

Best reading: budget ~0.3–0.7 s

Budget ~0.3–0.7 s for E2B create-to-first-command; self-host only beats it with a snapshot-restore warm pool (~28 ms restore), which is one of the 12–18 eng-months items. Cold start does not decide this question.

  1. “<125 ms” is VMM-to-guest boot on AWS’s tuned path; benchmarks time API call → network → scheduling → first exec, so they must be higher.

  2. 717 / 150 ≈ 4.8  → the “~5×” disagreement
717 / 264 ≈ 2.7  → gap between the two E2B measurements

    The 2.7× gap is unexplained (region, client location, method).

  3. Budget = range of the two measured E2B values, rounded: 264 → 0.3 s, 717 → 0.7 s.

Cold-start numbers disagree by ~5× (150 ms claimed vs 264–717 ms measured) because they measure different things; for agent UX the gap is small.

Self-hosted Firecracker: cold boot ~1.1 s, snapshot restore ~28 ms (Ryzen 7 / 32 GB). The fast path needs a snapshot-restore warm pool: one of the 12–18 eng-month items.

Risks & unknowns

  • Prices move fast: Hetzner raised twice in 2026; vendor rates may also change.
  • Concurrency limits, not price, may force the decision first (E2B Pro 100; Modal Starter 100).
  • Build-effort figures come from a vendor with a conflict of interest; no neutral estimate found.
  • Idle-heavy agents: billed-hours depend on pausing aggressively; a sandbox that “computes 10 minutes of each hour but lives the full hour costs 6×”.
  • Hetzner Cloud nested virtualization status is undocumented.

Most sensitive input: build eng-months. Move it from 4 to 12 in Conditions and break-even jumps to ~69k h/month. Second: ops-hours. Set it to 0 and you reproduce Side 1’s blog math.
