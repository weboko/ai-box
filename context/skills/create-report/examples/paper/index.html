Self-host Firecracker on Hetzner, or rent E2B / Modal?

A sandbox decision for a 2-person dev-tools startup — costs, cold starts and the labor nobody prices in

Claude Code† · model claude-opus-5-5 · October 18, 2026

†Research + build agent (remote session). Prices as of 2026-10-18; FX assumption 1 EUR = 1.16 USD.

Report metadata

Abstract

Rent (E2B first). Self-hosting only wins on hardware; once your own time is priced in, the break-even moves from ~500 to ~13,000–32,000 sandbox-hours a month. Two Hetzner AX42 boxes cost about as much as ~490 sandbox-hours/month on E2B Pro. But Firecracker itself is the easy part: networking, snapshots, scheduling and an in-VM agent are 12–18 engineer-months for a production platform, and ops labor dominates below a few thousand sandbox-hours a month. For a two-person team that is the whole company for half a year. Start on E2B (Firecracker isolation, pause/resume) or Modal Starter (cheapest below ~2,070 h/month); keep an exit path; re-check at ~$5k/month of sandbox spend.

Introduction

There are three ways to give an agent a disposable Linux box: E2B (Firecracker microVMs), Modal (gVisor containers), or your own Firecracker fleet. E2B runs each sandbox as a Firecracker microVM with its own kernel. Modal Sandboxes default to gVisor, a user-space kernel that intercepts syscalls; VMs are an alternative. Self-hosting means running Firecracker under its jailer on KVM-capable bare metal.

Summary of findings

  1. Hardware math favours self-hosting early. Break-even on hardware alone: ≈ 490 h/month. Break-even vs E2B Pro on hardware alone ≈ 490 sandbox-hours/month, consistent with an independent ~400 h/month estimate even after Hetzner’s 2026 price rises.
  2. Labor flips it. With labor priced in: ~13,000–32,000 h/month. Adding 20 ops-hours/month pushes break-even to ~13,000 h/month (~18 sandboxes running 24/7); adding a 4-engineer-month build pushes it to ~32,000 h/month.
  3. Hetzner got ~2× more expensive for new orders on 15 June 2026. AX42-1 is now €97.30/month + €49 setup; cloud CPX42 went €25.49 → €69.49. Blog break-evens using pre-June prices are stale.
  4. Hetzner Cloud VMs are not an option for Firecracker. Firecracker needs /dev/kvm. Firecracker needs /dev/kvm, which requires nested virtualization on cloud VMs; plan on dedicated AX servers, where KVM works. Hetzner does not document nested virt on Cloud.
  5. Cold-start numbers disagree by ~5× (150 ms claimed vs 264–717 ms measured). They measure different things; for agent UX the gap is small. See .
  6. Modal vs E2B is a fee-vs-rate trade. Crossover ≈ 2,070 h/month. Modal costs $0.238/h for 2 vCPU / 4 GiB vs E2B’s $0.166/h, but has no plan fee; E2B Hobby’s 1-hour session and 20-sandbox caps push real use to Pro at $150/month. Crossover ≈ 2,070 h/month, see .

Isolation

Firecracker provides a hardware virtualization boundary, with the jailer as “a second line of defense”. gVisor is stronger than namespaces and lighter than a VM. A self-hosted fleet gets E2B-grade isolation only if you keep the seccomp/jailer config tight — “a permanent line item”.

Plan limits

E2B Hobby: 1 h sessions, 20 concurrent; Pro: 24 h, 100 concurrent, $150/month; Enterprise $3,000/month minimum, BYOC available. Modal: default sandbox lifetime 5 min, max 24 h; Starter 100 containers, Team 5,000 containers at $250/month.

Trade-offs

Renting gives minutes to first sandbox and a linear bill; self-hosting gives a bill that is flat until a box fills but takes 12–18 engineer-months to reach production. compares nine criteria.

compares the three paths on nine criteria. The managed options differ mainly in isolation model, idle billing and GPUs; self-hosting differs in kind: it trades a linear bill for a flat one and a long build.

Architecture and build effort

contrasts the two paths. On the managed path the vendor owns the control plane, scheduler, template builds, snapshots, networking and observability. On the self-host path you own every box: eight components on top of jailer → Firecracker → KVM on a Hetzner AX dedicated server.(1) API and lifecycle, (2) scheduler/orchestrator, (3) template pipeline, (4) snapshot store, (5) per-sandbox networking, (6) in-VM agent, (7) observability, (8) governance, all on jailer → Firecracker → KVM on a Hetzner AX dedicated server, not a Hetzner Cloud VM, which has no documented /dev/kvm.

Costs

We price one reference sandbox of 2 vCPU / 4 GiB (E2B’s default is 2 vCPU / 0.5 GiB; 4 GiB matches the comparison in). It costs $0.1656/h on E2B and $0.2380/h on Modal; self-hosting is a fixed cost of ~$230/month for the minimum fleet, with ~$0 marginal cost until the box is full. plots monthly cost against usage.

At 2,000 h a month, E2B Pro costs $481, Modal Starter $476 and self-hosting all-in $4,897; the all-in break-even with E2B Pro is 32,150 h a month. These figures follow the inputs of .

Adding cost lines one at a time moves the break-even with E2B Pro: hardware only ~490 h/month; with ops labor ~13,260; with a 4 engineer-month build ~32,150; with a 12 engineer-month build ~69,230 (). Modal Starter is cheaper than E2B Pro below ≈ 2,070 h/month.

Conflicting estimates

Two published disagreements bear on the decision. We show both sides, then reconcile them.

When does self-hosting pay off?

One side puts break-even at ~400 sandbox-hours/month on cheap Hetzner hardware; the other says ops labor dominates below a few thousand and a production platform takes 12–18 engineer-months. Both are right about different cost lines. Repriced and with labor added, self-hosting pays only above ~13k–32k sandbox-hours/month.

Side 1

Break-even at ~400 sandbox-hours/month (“roughly one default sandbox running half the day”) on a ~€64 AX52 vs E2B; a companion post puts break-even near two dozen runs a day, using an 8-vCPU/16-GB Hetzner cloud box at ~$19/month and ~$0 marginal cost.

Side 2

Ops labor is “the dominant line below a few thousand sandbox-hours a month”; building a production platform is “comfortably 12–18 engineer-months” (the author sells a sandbox product: a conflict of interest).

Reconciliation, step 1 (price)

Side 1’s hardware prices predate Hetzner’s 15 June 2026 repricing for new orders: the $19 cloud box (CPX42-class) is now €69.49, and Hetzner Cloud lacks documented KVM anyway. Recomputed with 2 × AX42 at €97.30, with Cself the monthly self-host cost, F the E2B Pro fee and rE2B from :

H* = Cself − FrE2B = 230.47 − 1500.1656 ≈ 486 h/month

Side 1’s hardware conclusion survives the price hike.

Step 2 (labor). Side 1 prices labor at zero; Side 2 says labor dominates. Adding just 20 h/month at $100/h:

H* = 2,230.47 − 1500.1656 ≈ 12,560 h  →  with fleet sizing ≈ 13,260 h/month

Conclusion. Both are right about different cost lines. For a 2-person team, labor is not a rounding error — it is the scarcest input. Self-hosting pays only above roughly 13k–32k sandbox-hours/month, i.e. an E2B bill of ~$2.3k–5.5k/month.

“With fleet sizing” in : at higher usage the fleet grows past two boxes (peak = 2× average, 14 sandboxes per AX42), which raises Cself; recomputes the break-even in 10-hour steps with the fleet re-sized at each step.

How fast is a cold start really?

Firecracker claims boot “in <125ms” and E2B is commonly quoted at ~150 ms, but measured create-to-first-command times are 264–717 ms for E2B (). They measure different things; cold start does not decide this question.

Reconciliation

“<125 ms” is VMM-to-guest boot on AWS’s tuned path; benchmarks time API call → network → scheduling → first exec, so they must be higher. The 2.7× gap between the two E2B measurements is unexplained (region, client location, method). Budget ~0.3–0.7 s for E2B create-to-first-command. Self-hosting only beats it with a snapshot-restore warm pool (~28 ms restore), which is one of the 12–18 engineer-month items. Cold start does not decide this question.

717 ms / 264 ms ≈ 2.7  (LogRocket vs Lizard, E2B)

Recommendation

The recommendation below is the author’s judgement, built on the estimates above.

  1. Now: E2B Pro, or Modal Starter while under ~2,000 h/month.E2B Pro (or Modal Starter while under ~2,000 h/month and if 24-hour sessions/no-pause are fine). Choose E2B if you need pause/resume or microVM isolation for untrusted user code; Modal if you also need GPUs.
  2. Keep the exit open: a thin sandbox interface.wrap sandbox calls behind a thin interface (create / exec / files / pause / kill) so a switch costs days, not months.
  3. Re-evaluate when sandbox spend passes ~$5k/month for 3 consecutive months, you hit E2B Pro’s 100-concurrent cap, or a customer requires data residency / BYOC (E2B Enterprise starts at $3,000/month).
  4. If you do self-host later: dedicated AX servers, not Hetzner Cloud; budget the 8 components, not just Firecracker.

Risks and unknowns

  • Prices move fast: Hetzner raised twice in 2026; vendor rates may also change.
  • Concurrency limits, not price, may force the decision first (E2B Pro 100; Modal Starter 100).
  • Build-effort figures come from a vendor with a conflict of interest; no neutral estimate found.
  • Idle-heavy agents: billed hours depend on pausing aggressively; a sandbox that “computes 10 minutes of each hour but lives the full hour costs 6×”.
  • Hetzner Cloud nested virtualization status is undocumented.

Formulas

rE2B = vCPU × 0.0504 + GiB × 0.0162;   CE2B Pro = 150 + H × rE2B
rModal = (vCPU / 2) × 0.141912 + GiB × 0.024012;   CModal = H × rModal
hosts = max(2, ⌈(H / 730) × peak / ⌊56 / GiB⌋⌉)
Chw = hosts × (97.30 + 49 / amort) × fx;   Call-in = Chw + ops × rate + build × 160 × rate / amort

The break-even H* is the smallest H, searched in 10-hour steps with the fleet re-sized at each step, for which Call-in ≤ CE2B Pro. E2B Hobby applies only within 20 concurrent sandboxes and 1-hour sessions.
